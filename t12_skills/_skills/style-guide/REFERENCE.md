# Style Guide Reference

## Brand Voice at a Glance

| Attribute      | Do                                                        | Don't                                             |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Voice          | Sound like a knowledgeable, friendly colleague              | Sound like a legal disclaimer or corporate memo    |
| Tone           | Warm, direct, confident                                     | Overly formal, apologetic, or salesy               |
| Sentences      | Short, active-voice sentences                                | Long, stacked clauses with passive voice           |
| Vocabulary     | Plain, everyday words                                        | Jargon, buzzwords, internal acronyms               |
| Point of view  | "You" for the reader, "we" for the company                   | Third-person ("the user", "the company")           |
| Contractions   | Use them ("we're", "you'll", "it's")                          | Avoid stiff full forms ("we are", "you will")      |

## Content Types

### Emails
- **Subject lines**: keep under 50 characters; avoid "URGENT", ALL CAPS, or vague subjects like "Update".
- **Body**: one clear idea per paragraph; a single, obvious call-to-action (CTA) as its own short sentence or button
  label (e.g. "Reset your password"); sign off with a first name, not "The Team".
- **Tone dial**: transactional emails (receipts, confirmations) — brief and factual; marketing emails — energetic
  and benefit-led; support emails — empathetic and solution-first (see Support / Help Content below).

### Blog Posts
- **Structure**: one H1 title, H2 for major sections, H3 for sub-points; intro should state the takeaway in the
  first two sentences — don't bury the point.
- **Style**: aim for an 8th-grade reading level; spell out acronyms on first use.
- **Headings**: use sentence case (not Title Case); make headings outcome-focused ("Cut deploy time in half") not
  label-style ("Deployment Improvements").

### Social Media
- **LinkedIn**: aim for 1,300 characters or fewer; open with a hook in the first line (it's the only part shown
  before "see more"); use 3-5 relevant hashtags at the end; emojis sparingly, only to break up long posts.
- **Twitter/X**: stay within 280 characters per post; open with the key point immediately — no throat-clearing;
  hashtags optional and limited to 1-2; emojis welcome for tone but don't replace words with them.

### Support / Help Content
- **Tone**: empathy-first — acknowledge the issue before explaining the fix; solution-first — lead with what to do,
  not with an apology paragraph.
- **Language**: use partnering phrases ("Let's fix this together", "Here's what we'll do") instead of blame-shifting
  or purely instructional language.
- **Format**: numbered steps for any multi-step fix; bold UI element names (e.g. **Settings** > **Account**); close
  with an offer for more help ("Still stuck? Reply here and we'll take a look.").

## Grammar & Mechanics
- **Punctuation**: always use the Oxford comma; use an em dash (—) for asides, not double hyphens; avoid exclamation
  marks except in celebratory or urgent contexts, and never more than one in a row.
- **Capitalization**: sentence case for headings; capitalize product names exactly as branded (e.g. "GitHub
  Copilot", not "Github copilot").
- **Numbers**: spell out one through nine; use digits for 10 and above, and always for percentages, dates, and
  measurements (e.g. "5%", "3 days").
- **Inclusive language**: use gender-neutral pronouns ("they/them") when the subject's gender is unknown; prefer
  people-first terminology (e.g. "person with a disability" over "disabled person") unless a style-specific term is
  requested.
